# Market research: a share-first political compass

_Research date: 15 September 2026_
_Phase: desk research and live product audit; hypotheses still need user validation_

## Executive read

The current market is not one product category. It has at least three different jobs:

1. **Identity quiz:** “What kind of political person am I?” The World's Smallest Political Quiz gets there in 10 statements and about two minutes; 16Personalities is a much longer identity-profile benchmark.
2. **Election utility:** “Which party or candidate is closest to my views?” Vote Compass uses an election-specific questionnaire and a results dashboard.
3. **Ideology mapping and meme language:** Political Compass, 8values, SapplyValues, and PoliticalCompassMemes use charts, quadrants, axes, characters, and jokes as a shared visual grammar.

The opportunity is therefore not simply “make the old compass prettier.” It is to make a **fast, culturally fluent political self-reflection artifact** that people want to post, compare, and argue about—without pretending that a playful result is a scientific diagnosis or voting advice.

The initial product thesis:

> A two-to-four-minute, anonymous political self-reflection that produces a nuanced identity card people want to share, rather than a dot trapped in a four-quadrant chart.

This is a product hypothesis, not a proven market fact. The research supports the ingredients: young adults use Instagram and TikTok heavily; politics on TikTok is often encountered alongside humor and entertainment; existing tests prove that short completion and shareable results can work; and open-web communities already treat compass charts as remixable media. The combination still needs testing with real users.

## Scope and method

This brief combines:

- A live audit of [The Political Compass](https://www.politicalcompass.org/test/en?page=1), including a manual count of its six pages and 62 proposition items.
- Official product and methodology material from [Vote Compass](https://votecompass.com/) and [Vox Pop Labs](https://voxpoplabs.com/vote-compass/).
- Direct reviews of adjacent quiz, ideology, community, and sharing products.
- Current audience research, especially Pew Research Center's 2024–2026 work on young adults, TikTok, Instagram, social news, and influencers.
- The three user-provided screenshots: The Political Compass home page, the CBC Vote Compass landing page, and Google Images results for “political compass,” all captured 15 September 2026.

Published product metrics are marked **self-reported**. Reddit and image-search observations are directional cultural signals, not representative surveys. No interviews, usability study, or representative political-attitude survey has been conducted yet.

These signals do **not** prove that all Gen Z users want a political quiz. They support a narrower bet: if the product is aimed at social-first young adults, it should be discovered and consumed like a lightweight visual identity format, not like a civic-information portal.

### The key market gap

The shortest products are usually simplistic. The nuanced products are usually long. The election products are useful but situational. The meme ecosystem is expressive but does not guarantee a coherent measurement.

That leaves a testable middle:

**short enough for a feed, rich enough to reveal contradictions, and designed as a shareable artifact from the beginning.**

## Reference-site UX audit

### The Political Compass

The supplied screenshot shows a large desktop-first content site: a pale-blue vertical navigation rail, advertising, dense text, many secondary resources, and a classic branded quadrant system. The visual language is recognisable but not optimised for a first-time mobile visitor or a social feed.

The live test adds a more important product lesson. Its introduction explicitly frames the items as propositions rather than ordinary survey questions, says some are intentionally vague or biased, and says the goal is to trigger reactions and measure feelings or prejudices. Its FAQ also says the test is not an internet personality classification tool and that the scoring details are not released. [Official test introduction](https://www.politicalcompass.org/test/en?page=1) · [Official FAQ](https://www.politicalcompass.org/faq)

What is worth retaining:

- A clear, memorable visual model.
- Prompts that reveal instincts, not only policy positions.
- A result that feels distinct from a generic left/right label.

What to change:

- Replace six-page commitment with a fast core and optional depth.
- Make every prompt easy to understand on a phone.
- Explain how each answer moved the result.
- Make the result the centre of the experience rather than a certificate after a long funnel.
- Keep provocative wording from becoming ambiguity that users mistake for rigour.

### CBC Vote Compass

The supplied screenshot shows a much more polished institutional landing page: prominent CBC/Vox Pop Labs branding, a large election edition, one highly visible “Get started” action, response count, and a strong privacy/support footer. The presentation feels trustworthy and purposeful, but it is still an election utility.

The official methodology says Vote Compass deliberately limits the questionnaire to a finite set of accessible questions: more detail could improve distinctions but would hurt accessibility and usability. It normally uses about 30 final questions, a five-point response scale, and candidate/party positions grounded in public statements and documents. [Vote Compass methodology](https://www.voxpoplabs.com/votecompass/methodology.pdf)

What is worth retaining:

- A single obvious call to action.
- Clear privacy language near the entry point.
- Strong evidence behind claims about political positions.
- Multiple views of the result instead of one unexplained score.

What to change for this product:

- Remove election, postcode, candidate, and country setup from the first experience.
- Turn the result dashboard into a compact card/carousel that makes sense in a screenshot.
- Use a playful identity frame while keeping the methodology page serious.

### The supplied image-search results and the meme ecosystem

The Google Images screenshot is revealing: the familiar four-quadrant template is repeatedly filled with characters, politicians, quotes, media brands, games, and jokes. The chart works because people already understand its grammar. The creative opportunity is to keep that instant recognisability while giving the output more personality than a dot and more nuance than four labels.

The [PoliticalCompassMemes rules](https://www.reddit.com/r/PoliticalCompassMemes/) explicitly prefer compass content incorporated into a meme. [Compass Maker](https://developers.reddit.com/apps/compassapp) goes further by making axes, colors, labels, images, and poll mode editable. These are evidence that the share object can be the product—not merely an export button at the end.

## Sharing and no-backend feasibility

A Vercel-hosted static client is a good fit for the first phase. The core can run entirely in the browser:

1. Questions and scoring rules ship as static assets.
2. Answers are calculated locally.
3. A compact result is encoded into a URL, with local storage used only for a return visit if desired.
4. The browser renders a share card and can download it as an image.
5. Mobile browsers can invoke the [Web Share API](https://www.w3.org/TR/web-share/) for a user-initiated share, with copy-link and download fallbacks.

The [16 Values Test](https://16valuestest.com/about) is a live example of the browser-only/link-and-image pattern. Open Graph's [`og:image`](https://ogp.me/) can provide a generic preview for the site, but a unique server-rendered preview for every encoded result would require dynamic image generation or another hosted asset. That is a constraint to design around, not a reason to add a database.

The no-backend boundary means phase 1 should deliberately avoid:

- Server-persisted profiles or accounts.
- A live public leaderboard or aggregate “how everyone answered” map.
- Persistent friend groups with shared state.
- Personalized server-side social preview images for arbitrary result URLs.
- Analytics that silently collect political answers.

An encoded result URL is also a disclosure mechanism: anyone who receives the link may be able to see the encoded result. The shared fields must be obvious, minimal, and resettable.

## Product opportunity

### Proposed positioning

> **Not left. Not right. More you.**
> A quick political vibe check for people whose views do not fit one box.

The wording is only a direction. It should be tested for whether it feels inviting rather than dismissive of serious politics.

### Product principles

1. **Finish in one sitting.** Test a 12–16 question core, targeting two to four minutes. Offer an optional “go deeper” route after the first result rather than demanding all depth up front.
2. **Use tensions, not inherited labels.** Start with plain-language spectrums such as safety ↔ freedom, order ↔ disruption, local ↔ global, and systems ↔ self-reliance. These are candidate lenses, not a final ideology model.
3. **Show a shape, not only a quadrant.** A small number of transparent dimensions, contradictions, and strongest signals can communicate more than a single coordinate. Avoid adding axes merely to look sophisticated.
4. **Make the result emotionally legible.** Give users a memorable archetype or phrase, three strongest signals, one surprising tension, and the ability to open the underlying answers. Never present the archetype as a diagnosis.
5. **Design the share artifact first.** Generate a polished 1:1 card and 9:16 story card, with a short explanation, distinctive visual mark, and “this is where I land” copy. Let users download, copy a link, or use native share.
6. **Turn comparison into a lightweight loop.** A friend can open a URL and compare two result shapes locally. Do not create a server-backed group product until there is evidence that people want it.
7. **Earn trust visibly.** Publish the questions, scoring formula, dimension definitions, examples of ambiguity, and limitations. Say “self-reflection” rather than “scientifically accurate.” Do not recommend a party or tell someone how to vote.
8. **Protect political privacy by default.** No account, location, demographic questions, or response upload in the MVP. Keep calculations local and make the privacy model understandable in one screen.
9. **Keep the interface feed-native.** Large type, one prompt at a time, immediate feedback, fast loading, keyboard accessibility, reduced-motion support, and no advertising before completion.

## What not to copy

- The Political Compass's six-page forced commitment, hidden scoring, and unexplained combination of provocative ambiguity with authoritative-looking output.
- Vote Compass's election setup, postcode/candidate matching, and dashboard density when the job is personal identity and sharing.
- A four-quadrant chart as the only result.
- A raw percentage with no explanation of what it means or where it came from.
- A “viral” loop based on collecting political answers, public rankings, or outrage bait.
- Party or ideological labels that imply the product has discovered a user's true political identity.

## Testable hypotheses and experiments

| Hypothesis | Small experiment | Measure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A short core will outperform a long canonical test for social acquisition. | Prototype 10–12 versus 16 questions with the same scoring model. | Start-to-finish rate, median completion time, result-view rate, share/download rate. |
| Scenario cards feel more natural than abstract agree/disagree statements. | Test matched prompts in two formats: everyday scenario versus proposition. | Drop-off per item, answer time, “felt like me” rating, qualitative confusion. |
| A multi-signal shape is more shareable than a four-quadrant dot. | Show the same score as a quadrant, a tension profile, and a compact “identity card.” | Share intent, screenshot/download behavior, unaided recall, perceived nuance. |
| A memorable archetype increases sharing only if the explanation feels fair. | Test neutral archetype names plus plain-language descriptions against axis labels alone. | Trust, perceived accuracy as self-description, misinterpretation, share rate. |
| Comparison creates a stronger loop than a solitary result. | Add a local compare URL after the first result; do not persist either person's answers. | Second-person opens, compare completion, repeat sessions, reported willingness to send. |
| Story-shaped output fits discovery better than a single square. | Offer 1:1 and 9:16 cards from the same result. | Download/share selection and qualitative fit across Instagram, TikTok, X, and messaging. |

Suggested first validation set: 50–100 organic testers, eight to twelve short interviews or session replays, and a privacy-reviewed event funnel. Treat those numbers as directional product learning, not evidence about the population.

## Risks and guardrails

### Validity and political harm

Political answers are sensitive and self-reported. A playful score can be mistaken for a psychological or scientific classification. The test should describe tendencies in the selected answers, disclose its cultural scope, avoid “accuracy” language, and never claim to predict voting behavior.

Vote Compass's methodology is a useful boundary: it explicitly says it is not voting advice or a prediction, and its research treats individual responses as private while publishing only aggregate analyses. [Methodology](https://www.voxpoplabs.com/votecompass/methodology.pdf)

### Cultural scope

Questions that feel obvious in one country can be loaded or meaningless elsewhere. Start with a declared scope—such as “general political values, not a country-specific voter guide”—and test wording across languages and lived contexts before claiming global relevance.

### Privacy and link leakage

No database reduces exposure, but an encoded share URL can still disclose a person's result. Encode only what is needed to render the card, explain that the link carries the result, and provide a way to start over. Avoid third-party AI classification or silent demographic inference.

### Moderation and harassment

There is a difference between a generated result card and a public gallery of user-created political content. The first can stay unmoderated in phase 1; the second creates reporting, abuse, and political-adjacent moderation requirements. Do not add a public feed until the need is demonstrated.

### Intellectual property

Write original questions, labels, explanations, illustrations, and scoring. Do not copy Political Compass items, assets, scoring, or branded terminology. Its FAQ states that it treats the test and scoring as protected work. [Official FAQ](https://www.politicalcompass.org/faq)

## Phase 1 recommendation

Build a narrow prototype with:

- 12–16 original questions and a two-to-four-minute target.
- Four to six candidate lenses, validated before they become permanent axes.
- Local calculation only; no backend, database, login, location, or party matching.
- A result with a visual shape, strongest signals, one contradiction, and an inspectable explanation.
- Downloadable 1:1 and 9:16 share cards, encoded result links, and native-share/copy fallbacks.
- A compact methodology/privacy page.
- Instrumentation only if it can be privacy-preserving and does not collect raw political answers.

The next decision should not be “which colors should the quadrant use?” It should be a small format test: three candidate result systems, two question styles, and two share-card layouts. Lock the final axis model and question count only after observing completion, comprehension, trust, and sharing behavior.
